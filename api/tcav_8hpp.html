Detailed Description

Testing with Concept Activation Vectors (INT-6): how much a concept, given only by examples, pushes a model towards a class, and whether that's more than chance.

TCAV (Kim et al., ICML 2018) works in three steps:

  1. A concept activation vector (CAV). A linear classifier separates a layer's activations on examples of a concept (striped images, say) from its activations on random examples. Its normal, pointing towards the concept, is the CAV.
  2. Conceptual sensitivity. For an input, the directional derivative of a class logit along the CAV, at that layer: grad_layer(logit) . cav. Positive means moving the activation towards the concept raises the class.
  3. The TCAV score. The fraction of a class's inputs whose sensitivity is positive.

One score means little. A CAV trained against one random set can point anywhere that separates the two sets, and a meaningless direction scores anywhere between 0 and 1. So RunTcav() trains CAVs against several random sets, and does the same with random sets in place of the concept. A two-sided t-test between the two lists of scores says whether the concept differs from chance.

The models are ExplainerContext chains, whose gradients are available at every module's output. Language models aren't covered: they don't expose gradients at an intermediate layer.
