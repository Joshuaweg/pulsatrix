Detailed Description

Split conformal prediction (KS-3): prediction sets for a classifier and intervals for a regressor that contain the truth with a chosen probability, whatever the model (Vovk, Gammerman and Shafer, 2005; Angelopoulos and Bates, arXiv 2107.07511).

A model's probability of 0.9 doesn't mean it is right 90% of the time. Conformal prediction turns any model's scores into a guarantee. Score each example of a held-out calibration set by how badly the model fits it, and take the ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉-th smallest score, q̂. A new input's prediction set holds every answer that would score at most q̂, so it can be empty. If the calibration examples and the new one are exchangeable, the set contains the true answer with probability at least 1 − α, and at most 1 − α + 1/(n + 1).

The guarantee is marginal: it holds on average over inputs, not for each one or each group. Check the coverage of each slice with PerSliceError() on the misses (EvaluateCoverage()).

Scores:

  • LAC, 1 − p(true class) (Sadinle, Lei and Wasserman, 2019): the smallest sets on average, but they can be empty, and hard inputs are undercovered;
  • APS, the probability mass of the classes ranked at or above the true one (Romano, Sesia and Candès, NeurIPS 2020): larger sets for harder inputs, closer to coverage for each input;
  • for regression, the absolute residual |y − ŷ|, giving ŷ ± q̂, or for quantile regressors CQR's max(lo − y, y − hi) (Romano, Patterson and Candès, NeurIPS 2019), giving [lo − q̂, hi + q̂].

LAC sets and absolute-residual intervals match MAPIE 1.1.0's SplitConformalClassifier and SplitConformalRegressor; tests/conformal_test.cpp checks them against tools/generate_conformal_reference_values.py.
