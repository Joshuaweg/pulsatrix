    LRP for Diffusion Models (Design)¶

    Status: D1 and D2 are implemented (D1, D2); D3 is a design only. D1's split changed when it was tested: see D1 in pulsatrix. The work is tracked as the DIFF epic, and the evidence is in the research notes.

    A diffusion model doesn't produce its output in one forward pass. It starts from noise (or from a fully masked sequence) and runs the same network many times, each step refining the last. Every explanation method published up to 2026-10 explains one of those steps: DAAM sums attention maps, DifFRACT builds attribution graphs inside one Flux step, and DLIG runs Integrated Gradients at one partially unmasked state. None follows relevance from the final output back through the whole sampler. No LRP for diffusion has been published, and the AttnLRP author confirmed in 2025-08 that he hadn't tried it (LXT issue #36).

    This page sets out three rules that do follow it:

    • D1 for continuous samplers (DDPM, DDIM)
    • D2 for masked diffusion language models (MDLM, LLaDA-style, DPLM)
    • D3 for the adaLN conditioning that diffusion transformers use

    There are no reference numbers to match. Each rule therefore comes with the result that would show it's wrong, and with a set of checks that don't need a reference.

    The common idea: seed once, unroll the sampler¶

    The earlier objection was that diffusion "has no single conserved relevance seed per timestep". That's true for one step taken alone. The way around it is to seed relevance once, on the final sample, and treat the whole sampler as one deep network. A denoising step reads the previous state and adds a correction, so it is a residual block. The rules already used for residual connections, attention and recurrences then apply step by step.

    What doesn't fit the network picture is collected into named sinks instead of being dropped: injected noise, the timestep embedding, mask tokens, biases and the epsilon stabilizer. The sinks and the input relevance together add up to the seed. The ledger of sinks is part of every explanation: it shows how much of the output the explanation could trace and where the rest went.

    D1: trajectory LRP for continuous samplers¶

    A DDPM step computes

    x_{t-1} = c1_t · x_t  −  c2_t · ε_θ(x_t, t, c)  +  σ_t · z_t
          (skip)         (network)                 (injected noise)

    DDIM is the same step with σ_t = 0. The relevance arriving at x_{t-1} is split across the three terms. The skip share goes straight to x_t. The network share runs through ε_θ with its ordinary layer rules and lands on x_t, on the conditioning c and on the timestep embedding (a constant, so a sink). The noise share is the injected-noise relevance of step t: how much of the explained output that step's randomness decided. It is exactly zero under DDIM.

    The split: absolute-value ratio, not epsilon¶

    In early steps the skip and network terms are large and nearly cancel, and early steps are where class and layout get decided. When terms cancel, the epsilon rule's shares R · h_k / Σh grow without bound. The one published signed split of this kind (Arras et al. 2019, LSTMs) needed ε = 0.2 to be stable and then scored near random. Rewriting the step in terms of the predicted clean sample x̂_0 doesn't help: the cancellation just moves inside x̂_0.

    D1 therefore uses the absolute-value ratio split from LRP for ResNets (Otsuki et al., ECCV 2024):

    R_k = R · |h_k| / (Σ_j |h_j| + ε)        h_1 = c1·x_t,  h_2 = −c2·ε_θ,  h_3 = σ·z

    It conserves relevance exactly, and every share is bounded however much the terms cancel. The small ε only handles 0/0. It loses sign, so the signed fractions h_k / Σh are logged next to it. The signed epsilon rule, with ε scaled to Σ|h_k|, stays available as an option, and it reports the relevance it absorbs at each step.

    The weakness is that the split follows magnitude, not contribution. Two large cancelling terms each get about half, so the network's share in an early step says how big the terms were, not what the network decided. The checks below are there to catch this.

    The checks caught it. On a planted class-conditional DDPM (DIFF-5) the absolute-value split gave the class the wrong sign in 9 of 12 samples. The network term −c2·ε_θ opposes the skip term whenever the network removes noise, yet the split hands it a share with the sign of R, so everything that acts through ε_θ comes back flipped. Pulsatrix's default is now the signed ratio R_k = R · h_k · sign(Σh) / (Σ|h_j| + ε): each term keeps the sign of its contribution, every share is still bounded by |R|, and what the terms cancel, R · (1 − |Σh| / Σ|h_j|), goes to a reported sink. See D1 in pulsatrix.

    The seed: a scalar of x_0¶

    Seed a scalar computed from the final sample, not the raw noise prediction. The one reported attempt at gradient × input on DDPM (LXT issue #36) seeded the noise prediction. Its relevance sign followed the sign of the predicted noise, and its maps changed from run to run. Three seeds are offered:

    • a region of x_0
    • a reconstruction error ‖x_0 − x_ref‖² rooted at a reference, as in LRP for autoencoders (Kobayashi et al. 2023), which conserves exactly at the seed
    • a downstream classifier's logit on x_0, whose own LRP supplies the relevance

    The budget¶

    R(seed) = R(x_T) + Σ_t [ R_noise,t + R_time,t + R_cond,t + R_bias,t + R_ε,t ] + R_readout-bias

    Bias absorption and epsilon absorption are kept apart. AttnLRP warns that rules which push bias relevance back into the inputs to force conservation become unstable in ways that are hard to diagnose. Three more cases get explicit handling:

    • Classifier-free guidance (1+w)·ε(c) − w·ε(∅) is a linear layer over two forward passes. Both passes are propagated.
    • Fixed positional embeddings get their own sink.
    • Latent diffusion adds the VAE decoder as a final block with its own rules.

    Memory¶

    Only the trajectory is stored: each x_t and each z_t (or the exact RNG state). The backward sweep re-runs step t's forward pass and then its relevance pass, so memory stays at one step. The forward pass must be deterministic for this to work. If it isn't, the recomputed pass won't match the one being explained.

    What would show D1 is wrong¶

    • Relevance on the conditioning doesn't concentrate in early steps. Theory ("critical windows", Li & Chen 2024) and DifFRACT's measurements (the text stream's share falls from 89.9% at the first Flux step to 5.4% at the last) both predict that it should.
    • Explanations vary across sampler seeds as much as DAAM's authors feared when they called gradient attribution through the sampler intractable.

    D1 in pulsatrix¶

    include/pulsatrix/trajectory_lrp.hpp (DIFF-5) samples a continuous diffusion model, records the trajectory, and explains a scalar of the final sample back through it:

    #include "pulsatrix/trajectory_lrp.hpp"

NoiseSchedule schedule(100, 1e-4f, 0.05f);                // or NoiseSchedule::Cosine(T)
ConcatDenoiser model(net, 8, 8, {1.0f, 0.0f}, &cpu);      // net reads [x_t, embedding(t), class]
ContinuousSamplerConfig config;                           // DDIM, 50 steps, eta 0 by default
config.steps = 20;
ContinuousTrajectory t = SampleContinuousDiffusion(model, schedule, StandardNormal(8, 1), config);
TrajectoryRelevance r = ExplainTrajectory(model, t, RegionSeed(t.sample(), {0, 1, 2, 3}));
// r.x_T: relevance on the starting noise. r.condition: on the class, r.condition_by_step per step.
// r.hops: each step's split (skip, network, noise, absorbed), the signed fractions, and where the
// network's share landed. value = x_T + condition + time + noise + absorbed + leak, exactly.
    • Samplers. NoiseSchedule has the linear and the cosine schedule (Nichol & Dhariwal 2021), DDPM's step and DDIM's (ddim_coefficients, ddim_step), both as x_prev = skip · x_t − network · ε + noise · z. SampleContinuousDiffusion runs DDPM on every timestep or DDIM on evenly spaced ones, and records every state and every noise draw. The noise comes from its own Box-Muller over mt19937_64, so a seed gives the same trajectory on every platform.
    • Seeds. RegionSeed (a region of x_0), ReconstructionSeed (‖x_0 − x_ref‖²), or any relevance vector on x_0, such as a classifier's input relevance.
    • Splits. TrajectorySplit::SignedRatio (the default), AbsoluteRatio (the design's first choice) and SignedEpsilon. Every hop logs the signed fractions Σ R_i · h_k,i / s_i next to the split, so cancellation is visible whichever split runs.
    • The network. ContinuousDenoiser is the interface; ConcatDenoiser wraps a Module that reads [x_t, embedding(t), c] and splits its input relevance into x_t, the timestep embedding (a sink) and the conditioning. Its default rule is the epsilon rule with the bias in the denominator (TrajectoryNetworkRules()). Pulsatrix's usual pre-bias rule divides by x W alone, which a hidden layer's bias can leave near zero while the unit is active. Chained over 20 steps it blew up: conditioning shares up to 570 times the explained value.
    • The rerun check. Each step is recomputed from the stored x_t, z and coefficients before its relevance pass; if that doesn't reproduce the stored next state, the explanation throws rather than explain a different trajectory.

    What the tests show. - A one-step DDIM on a linear denoiser matches the hand computation for both splits. - Under DDIM the noise sink is exactly zero; under DDPM it isn't, except at the last step. The budget balances to 1e-5 in every test. - When the skip and network terms cancel to 0.1%, the absolute split gives each half, the signed epsilon rule gives each about 1000 times the relevance, and the signed ratio gives each half with its own sign and absorbs the 99.95% that cancels. - A planted class-conditional DDPM (8 values, the class sets the sign of the first 4 and nothing else; an MLP denoiser trained here; 20 DDIM steps; 12 samples, all of which follow their class). The ground truth is interventional: remove the class at one step only, rerun the rest, and measure how far region A moves. That effect grows from 0.3% of the region at the first step to 28% at the last. Under the signed ratio: - The class gets the region's sign in all 12 samples. Its relevance on region A (median 2.06) is about 7 times that on region B (0.29). Removing the class at every step moves A by 2.83 and B by 0.63: the model does use the class a little for B. - Per step, the class's relevance tracks the intervention: median Spearman 0.98 over the 20 steps, lowest 0.89. Averaged over samples it rises from 0.0008 of the value at the first step to 0.18 at step 18, against 0.0026 and 0.16 for the intervention. - The absolute split's sign is wrong in 9 of 12 samples, and its per-step relevance doesn't track the intervention (median Spearman 0.17). - The first falsifier doesn't apply here. In this model the class acts late, not early, and the explanation agrees with the intervention that says so. A model whose class is decided early, and the critical-window measurement of when it is decided (DIFF-10), are needed to test it. The planted model uses a linear schedule to β = 0.05 (ᾱ_T = 0.08), not the cosine one: the cosine schedule's ᾱ_T of 1e-4 makes the first DDIM step multiply the noise prediction's error about 100 times, which samplers hide by clipping x̂_0, and clipping isn't part of the step D1 splits.

    Not yet. Randomizing the denoiser, Integrated Gradients through the sampler (DIFF-8), classifier-free guidance, latent diffusion's decoder, and a real image model.

    D2: commitment-graph LRP for masked diffusion LMs¶

    A masked diffusion LM starts from a fully masked sequence. At each step one bidirectional transformer pass predicts every masked position, and the sampler commits some of them. In the standard carry-over samplers (MDLM, LLaDA), committed tokens are never revised, and no hidden state passes between steps. The committed tokens are the only channel from one step to the next, so chaining relevance through them misses nothing.

    Within a step¶

    Seed the logit of a token committed at step k and propagate with the AttnLRP rules. The bidirectional mask changes where relevance can flow, not the rules. Relevance lands on three kinds of input: prompt tokens, tokens committed at earlier steps, and [MASK] tokens.

    Across steps¶

    Relevance that reaches a token committed at an earlier step j is not an endpoint. It is re-seeded at step j, onto the logit that produced that token. The steps are walked in reverse. Each step seeds every token committed there, with the relevance accumulated for it so far, in one relevance pass: with the forward activations fixed, every rule in use (epsilon, identity, uniform, softmax) is linear in the incoming relevance. Steps that commit nothing need no pass. What reaches the prompt is the explanation. Relevance on [MASK] tokens has no earlier step to go to, so it is the mask sink of that step.

    Tokens committed in the same step can't attribute to each other, so the graph is a DAG layered by step. The top seed is the logits of the tokens being explained, each at the step that wrote it, summed, and each seeded with its own value, as LXT seeds a logit. DLIG and the attribution-contract paper explain the summed log-probability instead; its log-softmax isn't linear in the logits, so it can't be seeded on them the same way, and the logit is what the rest of pulsatrix's AttnLRP explains. DLIG found that a fixed reference string correlated with infill quality far better than the model's own tokens (r = 0.197 against 0.013), so a fixed-target seed is offered as a check.

    The commit is "the last write"¶

    The core assumption is that relevance passes unchanged through the discrete commit (an argmax, or a sample). The attribution-contract paper supports it: for a masked diffusion model whose unmasking order is replayed rather than recomputed, Integrated Gradients' completeness residual falls from 97% at 1 step to 18% at 256 steps.

    Not every sampler commits for good. DPLM's default sampler scores every position at every step, filled or not, by the model's confidence in the token it would choose there now, and masks the lowest-scoring ones, including positions it has already decoded. The masked positions that survive the cut take the new prediction; the filled ones keep their tokens. ReMDM also re-masks. So relevance on a token is re-seeded at the step that wrote the token the reading step saw. Under a carry-over sampler that's the commit step. Under a remasking sampler a position can be written several times, so different steps may relay to different writes of the same position, and a revoked position holds [MASK] and feeds the mask sink until it's written again.

    Limits¶

    • Which token, not when. Which positions get committed at a step is decided by a top-k over every masked position, so relevance from the committed logit explains which token was chosen, not why it was committed then. The model often settles on an answer well before committing it. Commit times therefore date decisions late.
    • Greedy decoding. Sampling can commit a low-probability token, so explanation runs use greedy decoding.
    • Leakage compounds. Every hop leaks a little, so the absorbed relevance is reported per hop.
    • The mask sink is large and moves. Mask tokens make up a third of attention-sink tokens in LLaDA and Dream, and sinks move between steps. Results are reported with and without sink positions.
    • DPLM zeroes mask embeddings. ESM-style token dropout zeroes the <mask> embedding at inference, so input-times-relevance rules give exactly zero relevance there. DPLM's mask sink is measured at the first residual-stream input instead.
    • Model-specific bookkeeping. Dream predicts position j from hidden state j−1, so its seed sits one position earlier. LLaDA's classifier-free guidance combines two passes linearly, like D1's.

    What would show D2 is wrong¶

    • Its prompt relevance disagrees systematically with Integrated Gradients from prompt to output under the same frozen commit order.
    • Deleting the most relevant prompt tokens (with the trajectory replayed) does no better than deleting random ones.
    • Patching the highest-relevance tokens moves later commits no more than patching low-relevance ones. This is a causal check in the style of TimeROME-DLM.

    D2 in pulsatrix¶

    include/pulsatrix/masked_diffusion_lrp.hpp (DIFF-3) implements D2 on the trajectories SampleMaskedDiffusion records:

    #include "pulsatrix/masked_diffusion_lrp.hpp"

MaskedDiffusionResult t = SampleMaskedDiffusion(MakeDenoiser(*model, &cpu), input, config);
EncoderLMStepModel step(*model, &cpu);             // AttnLRP with LXT's rules
std::vector<CommitmentTarget> span;                // the generated tokens to explain
for (int64_t p = 512; p < 544; ++p) span.push_back({p, std::nullopt});
CommitmentRelevance r = ExplainCommitments(step, t, model->config().mask_token_id, span);
// r.prompt: relevance per prompt token. r.mask: the mask sink per position. r.relayed: what
// passed through each generated token. r.hops: one ledger line per step, leak included.
    • Two modes. CommitmentMode::Graph re-seeds relevance at the step that wrote each committed token. CommitmentMode::StepLocal stops it there, which is what every published method does and what the graph is compared with.
    • The ledger balances exactly. value = prompt + mask + leak, plus what stopped on committed tokens in StepLocal. Each hop reports its own leak, so a pass that loses most of its relevance is visible.
    • One pass per step. Every target and every relayed token of a step is seeded together; steps upstream of nothing are skipped. A test on the tiny MDLM checks that explaining three targets at once equals the sum of explaining each alone, so the linearity this relies on holds for the real rules, not only on paper.
    • Fixed targets. CommitmentTarget::token explains a reference token's logit instead of the one the sampler wrote (DLIG's check).
    • Any model. StepRelevanceModel is the interface: a forward pass and a relevance pass. EncoderLMStepModel wraps an EncoderLM (MDLM, ESM-2).

    The checks replay the trajectory with its write order frozen: each step writes the same positions, each taking the model's most likely token for the changed sequence (ReplayMaskedDiffusion, ReplayCommitmentValue).

    • CommitmentDeletionCheck deletes prompt tokens (replaces them with the mask token) most relevant first, against random orders.
    • CommitmentPatchCheck leaves each committed intermediate token masked in turn and compares the change in the value with the relevance the graph relayed through it.
    • tools/explain/pulsatrix_diffusion_lrp runs all of it on an infill: mask a span of a text, refill it by confidence, explain the span, and print the ledger, the top prompt tokens and both checks.

    What the tests show. - On a hand-built linear model, whose relevance is exact, every number of a three-step chain matches the hand computation, leak zero. - On a two-layer model trained on a planted copy task (x0 x1 x2 x3 → y0 = x1, y1 = x1 + 1), the graph puts the relevance of both outputs on x1, more than twice any other prompt token's, for every prompt tried. The output committed first, y1, takes up to 2.3 of y0's relevance in the step-local explanation; the graph carries it on to x1. Deleting prompt tokens by relevance destroys the value far faster than random deletion. About half of each pass's relevance leaks into the head's and LayerNorms' biases, which the ledger reports.

    On mdlm-owt (1024 tokens of A Scandal in Bohemia, span [512, 544) masked and refilled in 32 steps on the GPU, 61 s to explain): - The infill is fluent (" had been very happy to see him again, and I was glad to see him again…"). Every relevance is finite. The value 646.7 splits into prompt 559.7, mask 119.8 and leak −32.9. The median leak per pass is 0.13 of its seed, far less than on the copy task. - Step-local stops 243.4 on committed span tokens. The graph carries it on, raising the prompt's share from 332.0 to 559.7. - The most relevant prompt tokens are the span's neighbours (" glad", " he", " but") and the earlier phrase " desire to see … again" (positions 381–385), which the infill echoes. 29.9% of |relevance| lies within 16 tokens of the span, which holds 3.2% of the prompt. - Deletion beats random: deletion AUC 348.8 for the graph and 335.9 for step-local, against 439.9 for random orders. The graph does not beat step-local here; its extra relevance moves onto tokens that matter about as much. - The patch check has nothing to test on an infill: every written token is a target, so no intermediate is left to patch. It needs a target set smaller than the trajectory, such as the last few tokens of a span.

    On DPLM-650M (proteins, DPLM's remasking sampler, --targets last so the patch check has intermediates to test, on the GPU): - Ubiquitin with residues [30, 46) masked, 16 steps: DPLM writes the span back exactly, with no re-masks. Deletion beats random (AUC 5.75 for the graph and for step-local, 7.98 random); the patch check doesn't (Spearman −0.25 over 15 tokens). - TEM-1 β-lactamase (1BTL, 263 residues) with [100, 148) masked, 16 steps: 30 of 48 residues as in the original, still no re-masks. Deletion: graph 1.80, step-local 2.07, random 5.90. Patching 45 tokens: Spearman 0.18. With 48 steps (one position per step) the Spearman is −0.13. - The golden GFP case (its first 95 residues, middle third masked, 32 steps): 3 re-masks, the same ones DPLM's own generate makes. The infill is a different protein, 1 of 33 residues as in the original, with a thioredoxin-like W C G P C motif. Every relevance is finite. The value 1.97 splits into prompt 0.51 and leak 1.46; one pass leaks −0.64 of its seed, the only negative leak in these runs. Deletion separates nothing here (graph −1.33, step-local −1.20, random −1.14): the value collapses after the first 10% of the prompt whatever the order. Patching 32 tokens: Spearman 0.21. - Re-masks are rare under greedy decoding. The scores of decoded positions seldom fall below those of masked ones, so of the inputs tried only the golden case re-masks. The relay through a re-masked write is checked exactly by the hand-built test; at this scale it rests on that one case. - Leak is far higher than on mdlm-owt: a median of 0.46–0.77 of each pass's seed goes to the biases and LayerNorms. Graph and step-local agree closely on every protein.

    On A2D-Qwen3-0.6B (dLLM's Qwen3-0.6B-diffusion-mdlm-v0.1, the same 1024 tokens and span as mdlm-owt, 32 confidence steps, --targets last, on the GPU; 89 s to sample, 305 s to explain, 97 min for prompt deletion and 44 min for patching): - The infill degenerates: " to a man, man,, man, man,, man, …". This is the model, not pulsatrix: the authors' modeling code, decoding the same window the same way (one position per step, the most confident), writes the identical 32 tokens. Its card samples in blocks of 64, which pulsatrix doesn't have yet. - Every relevance is finite. The value 17.0 splits into prompt 1.8, mask 0.3 and leak 14.9; the median leak is 0.85 of each pass's seed. - The most relevant prompt tokens are the span's neighbours (" rang", " bell", " me"); 35.2% of |relevance| lies within 16 tokens of the span, which holds 3.2% of the prompt. - Deletion: graph 5.39, step-local 5.80, random 8.48. Patching 31 tokens: Spearman 0.38, the only clearly positive patch result so far.

    Not yet. The comparison with Integrated Gradients under the same frozen commit order needs gradients at every step's input embeddings, chained through a soft relaxation of the committed tokens, and moves to DIFF-8 with DLIG.

    D3: adaLN and adaLN-Zero¶

    A diffusion transformer block with adaLN-Zero computes

    y = LN(x) · (1 + γ(c)) + β(c)
x_out = x + α(c) · Block(y)

    The modulation vectors γ, β and α come from a small MLP on the conditioning (the timestep, the class or the prompt). In a DiT the conditioning enters only here. So whether these products give relevance to the conditioning decides whether the explanation can say anything about the conditioning at all.

    AttnLRP's uniform rule splits a product half to each factor. It assumes the product is zero when both factors are, and LN(x)·(1+γ) isn't such a product. D3 therefore writes the modulation as a three-term sum, LN(x) + LN(x)·γ + β, and routes it with the same absolute-ratio split as D1. A switch then picks how the product term is treated:

    Component Split mode (explain the conditioning) Detach mode (explain the input only)
    LN(x) term to x through the LayerNorm identity rule the same
    LN(x)·γ term half to LN(x), half to γ → MLP → conditioning all to LN(x)
    β term to β → MLP → conditioning bias sink
    x + α·Block ratio split at the residual; α·Block split half and half ratio split; α held constant
    Sinks reported timestep, class or prompt bias only

    The precedents disagree on which mode is better. MambaLRP found that splitting a gate beats detaching it (1.978 against 1.577 on SST-2). Arras et al. found that, for LSTM gates, giving the gate nothing beats any split, which scored near random. adaLN differs from both, because its modulating factor is side information rather than the input being explained. Both modes are therefore implemented, and a planted test decides the default.

    Only some targets need D3. MDLM-owt was trained with time conditioning switched off, so its modulation vectors are constants. They fold exactly into the LayerNorm and projection weights at load time, and the folded model needs no D3. SEDD-small (which really uses its noise level) and image DiTs do need it.

    What would show D3 is wrong¶

    On a class-conditional DiT trained so that the class controls only one region of the image, split mode fails to send that region's relevance to the class embedding, or it scores near random on perturbation tests. Detach mode then becomes the default.

    Checks that need no reference¶

    Each check is cheaper than the next. A rule must pass all of them before any result is reported.

    Check Expected
    DDIM (σ = 0) The noise sink is exactly zero
    One step (T = 1) Reduces to ordinary single-network LRP
    fp64, biases zeroed, every factor detached Equals autograd gradient × input
    Uniform rule in fp64 without biases Each factor gets half of gradient × input

    Budget Input relevance plus every sink equals the seed, to fp32 tolerance
    Randomize only the denoiser The network's share changes. The skip share alone can survive, so a map that survives is not a pass
    Integrated Gradients through the sampler Totals agree with the budget (IG is complete)
